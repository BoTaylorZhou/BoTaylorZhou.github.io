  <h1>Rational inattention,<br><em>from theory to application.</em></h1>
  <p>Originating in the work of Nobel Prize laureate Christopher A. Sims, rational inattention explains how decision-makers optimally acquire and process information when attention is limited and information is costly. Our work studies both Gaussian and non-Gaussian models.</p>
</header>

      <article class="research-card"><div class="research-card__number">01</div><h2>RI for Transport</h2><p>Travellers make choices with incomplete information and limited attention. We model selective information acquisition about travel time, congestion, reliability, and charging availability, then connect these choices to transport network equilibrium.</p><p>Our goal is to develop scalable computational methods and understand how information provision, pricing, and transport policy shape attention and behaviour.</p><span class="research-card__meta">Networks · Choice · Policy</span></article>
      <article class="research-card"><div class="research-card__number">02</div><h2>RI for Control</h2><p>Autonomous agents face real constraints on sensing, communication, and computation. We treat information acquisition and transmission as part of the control problem itself.</p><p>Applications include formation control, cooperative navigation, collision avoidance, and task allocation for autonomous vehicles, UAVs, and robotic systems.</p><span class="research-card__meta">Agents · Control · Robotics</span></article>
      <article class="research-card"><div class="research-card__number">03</div><h2>RI for AI</h2><p>We use RI to model how intelligent agents allocate limited information-processing capacity across sensing, communication, memory, and computation.</p><p>The work focuses on selective information acquisition, attention allocation, state representation, multi-agent communication, and their effects on learning and decision performance.</p><span class="research-card__meta">Learning · Attention · Decisions</span></article>
